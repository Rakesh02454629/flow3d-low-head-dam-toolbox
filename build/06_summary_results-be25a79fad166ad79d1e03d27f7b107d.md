<h1>Summary of Results</h1>

<p>
After completing this training module, users should be able to generate and interpret the following outputs.
</p>

<h2>Main Hydraulic Results</h2>

<table>
  <thead>
    <tr>
      <th>Result</th>
      <th>Purpose</th>
    </tr>
  </thead>
  <tbody>
    <tr>
      <td>Water-surface elevation</td>
      <td>Identify flow depth, free-surface profile, and upstream/downstream water levels.</td>
    </tr>
    <tr>
      <td>Velocity magnitude</td>
      <td>Locate high-velocity zones and flow acceleration regions.</td>
    </tr>
    <tr>
      <td>Pressure distribution</td>
      <td>Evaluate pressure zones and hydraulic loading.</td>
    </tr>
    <tr>
      <td>Turbulence and vorticity</td>
      <td>Identify recirculation, roller structure, and energy-dissipation regions.</td>
    </tr>
    <tr>
      <td>Flow profiles</td>
      <td>Compare upstream, crest, toe, and downstream hydraulic conditions.</td>
    </tr>
    <tr>
      <td>Streamlines or pathlines</td>
      <td>Visualize flow direction and recirculation patterns.</td>
    </tr>
    <tr>
      <td>Animations</td>
      <td>Communicate transient flow evolution and hydraulic hazards.</td>
    </tr>
  </tbody>
</table>

<h2>Expected Products</h2>

<p>
Users should be able to produce:
</p>

<ul>
  <li>Plan-view velocity maps.</li>
</ul>

<ul>
  <li>Longitudinal velocity profiles.</li>
  <li>Cross-section plots.</li>
  <li>Water-surface elevation plots.</li>
  <li>Three-dimensional free-surface visualizations.</li>
  <li>Reverse-roller visualization figures.</li>
  <li>Simulation summary tables.</li>
  <li>Result comparison figures for different flow ranges.</li>
  <li>Animations for technical and public communication.</li>
</ul>

<h2>Interpretation Guidance</h2>

<p>
When reviewing results, users should consider:
</p>

<ul>
  <li>Whether the flow pattern is physically reasonable.</li>
  <li>Whether the simulation reached a stable or quasi-steady condition.</li>
  <li>Whether mass-conservation errors are acceptable.</li>
  <li>Whether mesh resolution is sufficient in the critical flow region.</li>
  <li>Whether the simulated water levels match available field or laboratory observations.</li>
  <li>Whether a reverse roller forms at the downstream toe.</li>
  <li>Whether the roller strength, recirculation zone, or surface return flow indicates potential public-safety concern.</li>
  <li>Whether structural modifications reduce hazardous flow behavior.</li>
</ul>
