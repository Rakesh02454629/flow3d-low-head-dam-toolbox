<h1>Toolbox for Each Audience </h1>

<p>

The CFD Toolbox for Low-Head Dam Hazard Analysis is designed to support different stakeholders in understanding, assessing, and mitigating hydraulic hazards associated with low-head dams, particularly the formation of reverse rollers and submerged hydraulic jumps.

</p>



<h2> Dam Owners and Operators </h2>



<p>

The toolbox helps dam owners and operators identify potentially hazardous flow conditions at low-head dams and understand how changes in discharge and tailwater levels influence the formation of reverse rollers. It supports informed decisions regarding dam safety assessments, hazard mitigation, operational practices, and prioritization of structural modifications to reduce drowning risks.

</p>


<hr>



<h2> Designers/Engineers</h2>



<p>

The toolbox provides technical guidance for developing, calibrating, validating, and applying Computational Fluid Dynamics (CFD) models to evaluate complex hydraulic conditions at low-head dams. It helps engineers assess flow characteristics, identify critical discharge and tailwater conditions associated with reverse roller formation, evaluate hydrodynamic forces and recirculation zones, and investigate potential design modifications and mitigation measures to improve hydraulic safety.

</p>

<hr>


<h2> Planners and Government Agencies </h2>

<p>
The toolbox supports planners and government agencies in identifying and assessing hydraulic hazards at low-head dams. It provides scientific information to guide public safety policies, hazard classification, prioritization of mitigation measures, infrastructure planning, and resource allocation to reduce drowning risks.

 </p>

 <hr>

 <h2> Recreational Users and Water Safety Advocates </h2>

<p>
  
The toolbox helps recreational users, including swimmers, kayakers, boaters, and anglers, understand the dangers of reverse rollers and hazardous flow conditions near low-head dams. It promotes hazard awareness, informed decision-making, and safer recreational activities through improved communication of hydraulic risks.

 </p>
